In 1954, a 28-year-old Hans Herrmann drove a 110-horsepower Porsche 550 Spyder across 3,000 kilometers of Mexico in the Carrera Panamericana — one of the most dangerous road races ever run. Car No. 55 won its class and finished third overall, outrunning cars twice its size.

Rähle's first car is a hand-built tribute to that Spyder — built piece by piece in Argentina, on a tubular steel-and-aluminum chassis, true to the proportions and spirit of the original.

Engineering

Real parts.
Real engineering.

01

Adjustable pedal box

The pedals slide forward and back on a track, so the same car fits a smaller driver today and a taller one next year — no refit required.

02

Speed limiter

A built-in limiter caps top speed while a new driver is learning. Turn it up as they get more confident behind the wheel.

03

Tubular chassis

A hand-welded steel-and-aluminum spaceframe — the same construction method used on real vintage race cars, not a toy shell over a go-kart frame.

04

Independent suspension

Coilover shocks at each corner, tuned to soak up a real dirt track instead of bouncing over it.

05

Real brakes

Hydraulic disc brakes, not a friction pedal — the car stops the way it should, every time.

06

Functional steering

A true steering rack connects the wheel to the front wheels directly, with the road feel of the original.
